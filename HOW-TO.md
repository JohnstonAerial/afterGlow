# Afterglow — How to use

Afterglow forecasts how good tonight's sunset or tomorrow's sunrise is likely to look, so you can decide whether to head out with the camera or drone.

🔗 [afterglow.johnstonaerial.com](https://afterglow.johnstonaerial.com)

## 1. Choose your location

Tap the location button near the top. You can:
- search by city, state or ZIP, or
- tap **Use current location** (your browser will ask permission).

Your choice is saved on your device. Change it any time with the same button.

If you open Afterglow from the **Drone Preflight Checklist** (the AfterGlow link under the golden hour times), it opens at the flight site you were checking there. If you have not picked a place in Afterglow yet, it keeps that one. If you already have, Afterglow shows the flight site for that visit only and your own place stays as it was.

## 2. Read the Today view

Use the **Sunrise** / **Sunset** toggle to switch between the two. You'll see a score and label, golden and blue hour times, a short plain-language explanation, and moon conditions.

| Label | Score | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Epic | 85+ | The sky should put on a show |
| Great | 70–84 | Strong color likely |
| Good | 44–69 | Decent color possible |
| Fair | 26–43 | Modest, mostly gradient |
| Flat | under 26 | Little to catch the light |

## 3. Plan ahead with Outlook

The **Outlook** tab lists the next seven sunrises and sunsets, color-coded by score, so you can spot the best day of the week.

## 4. See the sun's direction on the Map

The **Map** tab shows where the sun will be, by compass direction and height, at any point in the day. Use the arrows to change the day and the ⌖ button to re-center on your location. It's handy for choosing where to stand or fly.

## 5. Plan a shoot for any date or place

The **Plan** tab works for any date and any place, and it's separate from your saved location, so your alerts stay put.

1. Tap the place button to search a town or paste coordinates from Google Maps (for example `35.7672, -78.6631`). Places you've used before are listed under **Recent places**.
2. Pick the date with the arrows or the calendar. The map shows where the Sun and Moon rise and set that day.
3. Tap the map to drop a pin on what you want to shoot, and give it a name. You'll see its direction and distance, and how the Sun and Moon sit against it: behind it (backlit), to the side, or behind you (front-lit). Drag the pin to move it, or switch **Tap the map to place** to **My spot** to move where you're standing.
4. Under **Find the dates it lines up**, choose Sun or Moon to see the dates when it rises or sets behind your subject, or lines up with it.
5. Drag the **Time of day** slider to watch the Sun and Moon move across the sky, or tap Sunrise, Golden hr, Sunset or Moonrise. The **Sky view** at the bottom has its own slider that moves together with this one, so you can drag it and watch the Sun and Moon dots on the dial without scrolling back up.
6. **Sky outlook** scores the sunrise and sunset when the date is within about ten days. Further out, directions and times are still exact, but there is no sky forecast.
7. **Share this plan** copies a link that reopens the same place, subject, date and time. **Add to calendar** downloads a calendar entry (or use **Google Calendar**), with the light times in the notes. You can change the title and add your own notes first.

8. **Viewing from** sets Ground or Drone and a height above takeoff (it starts at 400 ft; use the buttons or type your own). The **Sun at the horizon** card then shows sunrise and sunset from the ground and from that height. From the air the horizon drops a little, so the Sun stays visible about a minute or two longer.
9. **Things in the way at this spot** lets you add trees, buildings or a hill: what it is, the compass range it covers, how tall it is and how far away. The app works out how much sky each one blocks from the ground and from your drone height, and shows when the Sun is lost behind it at sunset or clears it at sunrise. Entries are saved on your device with that spot.
10. **Print a shoot card** makes a one-page card with the compass, light times, subject, height, things in the way, your notes and a QR code that reopens the plan. To keep it as a PDF:
    - **iPhone or iPad:** the print screen opens. Tap the share icon at the top to save a PDF, or the blue print button to print.
    - **Mac:** in Safari, click **PDF** at the bottom left of the print window, then **Save as PDF** (untick Print headers and footers to leave off the date and web address Safari adds). In Chrome, set **Destination** to **Save as PDF**, then click Save.
    - **Android:** set the destination at the top to **Save as PDF**, then tap the PDF button.
    - **Windows:** set the printer or destination to **Save as PDF**, then save.

   The app shows the line that fits your device under the button. The QR code opens the plan in any web browser. It carries the place, date, time and subject pin, but not your notes, height or entries.

Plan doesn't know the real horizon: hills, tree height and buildings are only what you enter. The subject's own height isn't known either.

## 6. Add it to your Home Screen

- **iPhone / iPad (Safari):** tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the menu, then **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**.

It then opens like a normal app and works offline for the app itself. Forecast data is always fetched fresh when you're online.

## 7. Turn on notifications (optional)

1. Open Afterglow **from your Home Screen icon**. Notifications won't turn on from a regular browser tab.
2. Tap the 🔔 bell.
3. Choose what you want: sunrise, sunset, morning golden hour, evening golden hour.
4. Choose how far ahead: 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour or 90 minutes.
5. Tap **Save** and allow notifications when asked.

To stop, open the bell again and turn the alerts off. Your details are then removed from the notification service.

## Good to know

- If the forecast can't load, Afterglow shows the last one it got, clearly marked as old, with a **Refresh** button.
- Forecasts change as new weather model runs arrive. Check again closer to the time.
- Scores are a guide to the odds and are still being refined against real shoots.
