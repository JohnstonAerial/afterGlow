# Afterglow — How to use

Afterglow forecasts how good tonight's sunset or tomorrow's sunrise is likely to look, so you can decide whether to head out with the camera or drone. It also helps you plan a shoot: where the Sun and Moon will be, at any time, on any date.

🔗 [afterglow.johnstonaerial.com](https://afterglow.johnstonaerial.com)

The app has three tabs: **Today**, **Outlook** and **Plan**.

## 1. Choose your location

Tap the location button near the top. You can:
- tap **My location** (your browser will ask permission), or
- search by city, state or ZIP, or type coordinates (decimal, degrees/minutes/seconds, survey or aviation style, or a pasted Google or Apple Maps link).

Your choice is saved on your device. If you tap **My location** again later it says **Update my location**, and your place only changes when you tap it, never on its own.

## 2. Read the Today view

Use the **Sunrise** / **Sunset** toggle at the top to switch between the two. Use the ‹ and › arrows to move to the day before or after. You'll see a score and label, golden and blue hour times, a short plain-language explanation, and moon conditions.

| Label | Score | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Epic | 85+ | The sky should put on a show |
| Great | 70–84 | Strong color likely |
| Good | 44–69 | Decent color possible |
| Fair | 26–43 | Modest, mostly gradient |
| Flat | under 26 | Little to catch the light |

**Reality check.** In the last 3 hours before sunrise or sunset, a card appears on Today. It compares the forecast with recent airport weather reports toward the sun (airports see cloud below about 12,000 ft) and with a satellite view of high cloud, which is the layer that gives color. It tells you whether the airports agree with the forecast. It shows the sky now, not at sunrise or sunset, and it does not change the score. Outside that window a small note says when it will appear. Tap the card's title to collapse it.

**Refresh.** Under the Sunrise / Sunset toggle, Today shows when the forecast was fetched. Tap **Refresh** to load it again; you stay on the same day and sunrise or sunset.

**Other time zones.** If you look at a place in a different time zone, the times on Today are that place's local time, not your device's, and a note says so.

## 3. Look ahead with Outlook

The **Outlook** tab lists the coming sunrises and sunsets in order, color-coded by score, so you can spot the best day. It looks 10 days out; days 8 to 10 have a dashed outline because forecasts that far ahead are less certain. A Flat or Fair day shows a short reason underneath. Times that have already passed are dimmed, and "best" only points at one still ahead. Tap any row to open that day in Today.

## 4. Plan a shoot with Plan

The **Plan** tab is for working out where the light will be. It uses pure sun and moon geometry, so it works years ahead and anywhere in the world. Nothing here needs a weather forecast.

- **Planning from** shows the place you're planning for. Tap it to search for another place, use **My location**, or go back to your own place. Plan can look at any place without changing the one Today, Outlook and your alerts use.
- **Date**: pick today or any later day with the date row.
- **The map**: tap it to drop a pin. Use **Tap the map to place** to choose whether the next tap sets your **Subject** (what you want to shoot, such as a water tower) or **Your spot** (where you will stand or fly from).
- **Full screen**: tap **⤢** on the map to open it full screen. The time slider, **Now**, quick jumps and the **Subject / Your spot** switch are along the bottom, so you can move the time and watch where the Sun and Moon lines fall. Tap **✕** to close it. The small map re-fits to show your spot and your subject, and the **⌖** button returns to the place you were planning from before you dropped a pin.
- **The time bar** stays at the top as you scroll. Drag the slider, or tap **Now**, to move the Sun and Moon lines on the map and everything below it. It's the most useful control on the page: slide it and watch the picture and the readouts change.
- **View from your spot**: right under the time bar, a flat-horizon picture of where the Sun and Moon will be at that time, facing your Subject (or the nearer of sunrise or sunset if you haven't dropped one). A tag on the edge tells you when one is off to the side or high overhead. Along the top of the card, the **Jump to** buttons (sunrise, golden hour, sunset, moonrise, moonset) take the time bar straight to that moment; swipe the row sideways to see them all. Trees, buildings and other things you add under Things in the way are drawn at their real size, and the Subject can be given a shape with "Looks like" (water tower, bridge, lighthouse, wind turbine, city skyline, mountain or ridge, smokestack and more).
- **Height & things in the way**: just below the picture. Say how high you will be (drone or ground) and add trees, buildings, hills, towers or walls, and Afterglow will tell you how much they change what you see. Pick what each one is and it is drawn in the picture.
- **Subject**: shows its direction and distance from you, and how the light will fall on it at sunrise and sunset (for example front-side light or side light). You can give it a name. **Find the dates it lines up** is closed until you want it. Open it to find the dates when the Sun or Moon rises or sets in line with your subject.
- **Sky forecast**: shows how the sky is expected to look for the sunrise and sunset on that date, when the date is within about ten days. It sits close to the picture so you can read it as you change the date.
- **Sky view** (closed section near the bottom) shows the Sun and Moon on a compass from where you stand, north up. Closer to the middle is higher in the sky.
- **Share, calendar & print**: copy a link to the plan, or add the shoot to your calendar. Choose **Sunset shoot**, **Sunrise shoot** or **Custom**. With Custom you set the start relative to the Sun or Moon (for example, 2 hours before sunset, or 30 minutes before moonrise) or at a clock time, or use the time on the bar. Then choose how long it lasts. The link also carries your Things in the way, the Subject's shape, your height and the shoot window (not your notes). You can also print a shoot card with the compass (things in the way shown as wedges), the Subject's distance, a horizon picture at sunrise or sunset and a code that reopens the plan. Notes over 200 characters print on page 2.

On a computer, scrolling over the map scrolls the page. To zoom the map, use the **+** and **−** buttons, or hold ⌘ (Mac) or Ctrl (Windows) while you scroll or pinch. On a phone or tablet, one finger scrolls the page and two fingers move the map. In full screen, one finger moves the map.

## 5. Add it to your Home Screen

- **iPhone / iPad (Safari):** tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the menu, then **Install app** or **Add to home screen**.
- **Mac (Safari, macOS Sonoma 14 or newer):** click the Share button in the toolbar, then **Add to Dock**.
- **Mac (Chrome):** click **Install** at the right end of the address bar, then **Next**, then **Install**. A Finder window opens showing the app. Drag it to your Dock or Desktop to keep it handy. (If you don't see **Install**, open the menu and choose **Cast, save and share → Install page as app**.)
- **Windows (Chrome):** click **Install** at the right end of the address bar and follow the prompts. (Or open the menu and choose **Cast, save and share → Install page as app**.)
- **Windows (Edge):** click the **App available** icon at the right end of the address bar, then **Install**. (You can also open the menu, then **Apps → Install this site as an app**.)

Firefox on a computer can't install web apps, so alerts won't work there. Use Chrome, Edge or Safari for that.

It then opens like a normal app and works offline for the app itself. Forecast data is always fetched fresh when you're online.

## 6. Turn on notifications (optional)

Alerts only work when Afterglow is installed as an app (step 5). They won't turn on from a regular browser tab.

1. Open Afterglow **from its Home Screen or Dock icon**.
2. Tap the 🔔 bell.
3. Choose what you want: sunrise, sunset, morning golden hour, evening golden hour.
4. For sunrise and sunset, you can choose **Only if the forecast is** Good or better, Great or better, or Epic only. Afterglow checks the forecast at the moment an alert would go out. If the sky won't be good enough, you're not bothered at all.
5. Choose how far ahead: 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour or 90 minutes.
6. Tap **Save** and allow notifications when asked. The top of the bell window says which place your alerts are for. If you later change your location, open the bell and tap **Save** again to move your alerts to the new place.

When an alert has a minimum, the message includes the forecast, for example "Sunset in 60 minutes: Good (52)". To stop, open the bell again and turn the alerts off. Your details are then removed from the notification service.

## 7. Larger text

Tap **Aa** near the top to make all the text bigger. Tap it again to go back. Afterglow remembers your choice in that browser or installed app. Each one keeps its own setting, so if you use Afterglow in Safari and from your Home Screen, set it in both.

## 8. Light or dark (Theme)

Tap the theme button near the top (next to **Aa**) and choose **Auto**, **Light** or **Dark**. Auto follows your phone or computer setting, so Afterglow switches along with it. Afterglow remembers your choice in that browser or installed app. The **?** button opens the README. In dark mode the page has a soft gold glow along the right edge; in light mode it has a pale dawn-sky wash with the same glow.

## 9. Open the Drone Preflight Checklist for your place

When the sky looks good, check that you can fly there. The blue **Drone Preflight Checklist** button on **Today** (under Moon & timelapse) and on **Plan** (under the Sky forecast) opens the free checklist with the weather, airspace links and preflight checks for the same place. The button says which place it will use. On **Plan** it uses the place you're planning from, so you can check a spot before you go.

- **On a computer**, it reuses one tab, so repeated taps don't pile up new tabs.
- **On a phone or tablet in a browser**, it opens in the same tab. Tap **Back** to return. Afterglow remembers your tab and place.
- **In a Home Screen app**, it opens over Afterglow. Tap **X** to come back.
- **The place travels in the link itself.** If you use **My location**, the button opens the checklist for where you are, and says so.

The checklist has an **AfterGlow** button that does the reverse: it opens Afterglow at the checklist's place, on **Today**. Your Plan tab keeps its own place.

## Good to know

- If the forecast can't load, Afterglow shows the last one it got, clearly marked as old, with a **Refresh** button.
- Forecasts change as new weather model runs arrive. Check again closer to the time.
- Times and directions are calculated for a flat horizon, so hills and buildings change what you see.
- Scores are a guide to the odds and are still being refined against real shoots.
